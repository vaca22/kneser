\section{General real saddle-node unfoldings}\label{sec:general}

Sections~\ref{sec:transition}--\ref{sec:char} use the exponential only through a
few constants.  This section states their results for a general real
saddle-node unfolding, lists the changes in the proofs, and adds one exact
invariance statement.  Tetration is the case $a_2=\tfrac12$, $\gamma=1$,
$\rho=\tfrac13$ below, in the coordinate $w=\eee(1+u)$ (so $c=\eee$ below) and
with the parameter $\mu=\eee\log b$, $\mu_0=1$.

\subsection{Setting}
Fix an affine coordinate $u$ centred at the saddle-node, $w=w^*+c\,u$ with a
constant $c>0$, and write maps in this coordinate as
$f_\mu(u)=(f_\mu(w^*+cu)-w^*)/c$.
Let $f_\mu(w)$ be a family with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(H1)] $f_\mu(w)$ is entire in $w$, holomorphic in $\mu$ near $\mu_0$, and
  real for real $(\mu,w)$.
\item[(H2)] $f_{\mu_0}(w^*)=w^*$, $f_{\mu_0}'(w^*)=1$ and
  $a_2:=c\,f_{\mu_0}''(w^*)/2>0$ (replace $w$ by $-w$ if necessary).
\item[(H3)] $\gamma:=c^{-1}\partial_\mu f_\mu(w^*)|_{\mu_0}>0$ (reverse $\mu$ if
  necessary).  For $s:=\mu_0-\mu>0$ small there are two real fixed points
  $L<L_2$ near $w^*$, $L$ attracting with multiplier $\lambda$ and $L_2$ repelling
  with multiplier $\lambda_2$.
\item[(H4)] The base point $w_0<w^*$ is real, and $f_{\mu_0}$ is increasing with
  $f_{\mu_0}(w)>w$ on $[w_0,w^*)$.  Then for $s$ small, $w_0<L$ lies in the
  immediate basin of $L$, and the Koenigs map satisfies $\sigma(w_0)<0$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-real}).
\item[(H5)] $B_1\ne0$, where $B_n$ are the Fourier coefficients of the inverse upper
  horn map of the germ at $s=0$ (used only where indicated).
\end{enumerate}
Write the germ as $g(u)=(f_{\mu_0}(w^*+cu)-w^*)/c=u+a_2u^2+a_3u^3+\cdots$, with
iterative residue $\rho=1-a_3/a_2^2$.  Its formal Abel function is
$\alpha(u)=-1/(a_2u)+\rho\log(-u)+\sum_{k\ge1}d_ku^k$, and $\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}$,
$\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}$, $B_n$ are defined as in Section~\ref{sec:horn}.  The
normalisation $F(0)=1$ becomes $F(0)=w_0$: $\Rr^{-1}(w_0)=0$,
$u^*:=(w_0-w^*)/c$ and $a:=\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}(u^*)\in\mathbb R$.

The choice of $c$ does not matter.  Replacing $c$ by $c'$ conjugates the germ
by the linear map $\psi(v)=(c'/c)v$; then $\rho$, $a_2\gamma$ and the multipliers
are unchanged, while the Fatou coordinates become
$\Phi\circ\psi-\kappa$ with the real constant $\kappa=\rho\log(c'/c)$.  Hence
$B_n\mapsto B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii n\kappa}$ and $a\mapsto a-\kappa$, so
$B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}$ and $|B_n|$ do not depend on $c$.  More generally, for a real
germ $\psi(v)=c_\psi v+O(v^2)$ with $c_\psi>0$, the Fatou coordinates of
$\psi^{-1}\circ g\circ\psi$ are $\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}\circ\psi-\kappa$ and
$\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}\circ\psi-\kappa$ with
$\kappa=\rho\log c_\psi+\psi''(0)/(2a_2c_\psi^2)\in\mathbb R$: both functions satisfy the
Abel equation of the conjugated germ, and their difference from its formal Abel
function tends to $\kappa$ along orbits.  So $B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}$ is unchanged
under real-analytic conjugacy of the germ, with the base point transported.

The definitions of
$S$, $T$, $t_n$, $\tau_n$, $h$, $\Lambda$, $\Kk^W$ and $\mathcal C(Y)$ are those of
Sections~\ref{sec:transition} and~\ref{sec:char}, with $\mu$ in place of $b$.

No global hypothesis enters.  All estimates take place in a fixed disc about
$w^*$; (H1) is used only to make $S$ entire.

\begin{proposition}[Scales]\label{prop:gen-scale}
As $s\to0^+$,
\[
 |\log\lambda|,\ \log\lambda_2=2\sqrt{a_2\gamma s}\,\bigl(1+O(\sqrt s)\bigr),\qquad
 \Lambda=\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi^2}{\sqrt{a_2\gamma s}}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
and $p:=|\log\lambda|\log\lambda_2$ is holomorphic in $s$ with
$p=4a_2\gamma s+O(s^2)$.  Moreover, with $A_1=1/\log\lambda$, $A_2=1/\log\lambda_2$
and $u_1=(L-w^*)/c$, $u_2=(L_2-w^*)/c$,
\[
 A_1+A_2\to\rho,\qquad A_1(u_1-u_2)\to1/a_2,\qquad h_2-h\to2\pi\rho,
\]
where $h_2=2\pi/\log\lambda_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the Weierstrass preparation~\eqref{eq:weier} as
$f(u)-u=(u^2-\sigma_1u+\sigma_2)k_s(u)$ with $k_0(0)=a_2$.  Then
$\sigma_2=f_s(0)/a_2+O(s^2)=-\gamma s/a_2+O(s^2)$, so the discriminant is
$D(s)=4\gamma s/a_2+O(s^2)$, and $\lambda_{1,2}=1\mp a_2\sqrt D+O(D)$.  The
limits of $A_1+A_2$ and $A_1(u_1-u_2)$ are those of Lemma~\ref{lem:res}, with
$1-k'(0)/k(0)^2=1-a_3/a_2^2$ and $1/k(0)=1/a_2$.  Finally
$h_2-h=2\pi(A_1+A_2)$.  For tetration this gives $C_\eta$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:saddle} and $p=2s+\cdots$.

Holomorphy of $p$.  Shrink $r$ and $s_0$ so that $|f_s'(u)-1|\le\tfrac14$ on
$\{|s|<s_0\}\times\overline{D_r}$ and both roots of $q_s$ lie in $D_{r/2}$.
For $\phi(s,u)$ jointly holomorphic there, the power sum
\[
 \phi(s,u_1(s))+\phi(s,u_2(s))=\frac1{2\pi\ii}\oint_{|u|=r}\phi(s,u)\,
 \frac{q_s'(u)}{q_s(u)}\,du
\]
(roots counted with multiplicity) is holomorphic in $s$, since $q_s$ has no
zeros on $|u|=r$.  Apply this to $\ell=\operatorname{Log}f_s'$ and to $\ell^2$.
For real $s>0$, $\log\lambda_j=\ell(s,u_j)$, so
\[
 p=-\ell(s,u_1)\,\ell(s,u_2)
 =\tfrac12\Bigl[\bigl(\ell(s,u_1)^2+\ell(s,u_2)^2\bigr)-\bigl(\ell(s,u_1)+\ell(s,u_2)\bigr)^2\Bigr]
\]
extends holomorphically to $|s|<s_0$, with $p(0)=0$.  By the first display,
$p(s)/s\to4a_2\gamma$ as $s\to0^+$, so $p'(0)=4a_2\gamma$ and
$p=4a_2\gamma s+O(s^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Real dynamics]\label{lem:gen-real}
Assume \textup{(H1)--(H4)}.  For $s>0$ small, $f_\mu$ is increasing on
$[L,L_2]$ with $f_\mu(w)<w$ on $(L,L_2)$, so $(L,L_2)$ lies in the immediate
basin of $L$.  Moreover $w_0<L$, $f_\mu$ maps $[w_0,L)$ into itself with
$f_\mu(w)>w$ there, and $w_0$ lies in the immediate basin of $L$.  The Koenigs
map $\sigma$ of $L$, $\sigma'(L)=1$, is negative on $[w_0,L)$ and positive on
$(L,L_2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We work in the coordinate $u$; since $c>0$, order and signs are the same as in
$w$.  Shrinking $r$ and $s_0$, in~\eqref{eq:weier} we may assume
$|k_s-a_2|\le a_2/2$ and $|f_s'-1|\le\tfrac14$ on $D_r$.  For real $s>0$ the
discriminant $D(s)=4\gamma s/a_2+O(s^2)$ is positive, so $u_1<u_2$ are real and
$|u_j|\le C\sqrt s$; on $D_r\cap\mathbb R$, $f_s$ is increasing and $k_s>0$.

On $(u_1,u_2)$, $q_s<0$, so $f_s(u)<u$, while $f_s(u)>f_s(u_1)=u_1$.  Orbits in
$(u_1,u_2)$ therefore decrease to a fixed point in $[u_1,u_2)$, i.e.\ to $u_1$;
the connected set $[u_1,u_2)$ lies in the basin and contains $u_1$.

Take $\delta\in(0,r)$ with $g(-\delta)<-\delta/2$.  By \textup{(H4)}, $g(u)-u$ has a
positive minimum $m$ on the compact interval $I=[u^*,-\delta]$, and since $g$ is
increasing, $g(I)\subset[u^*+m,-\delta/2]$.  By uniform convergence $f_s\to g$
on $I$, for $s$ small $f_s(u)>u$ on $I$ and $f_s(I)\subset(u^*,-\delta/4)$; and
$-\delta/4<u_1$ because $|u_1|\le C\sqrt s$.  On $[-\delta,u_1)$, $q_s>0$, so
$f_s(u)>u$, and $f_s(u)<f_s(u_1)=u_1$ by monotonicity.  Hence $f_s$ maps
$[u^*,u_1)$ into itself with $f_s(u)>u$; orbits increase to a fixed point in
$(u^*,u_1]$, which can only be $u_1$.  As before $[u^*,u_1]$ lies in the
immediate basin.

The map $\sigma$ is real and increasing near $L$ with $\sigma(L)=0$.  For
$w\in[w_0,L)$, $f^n(w)\uparrow L$, so $\sigma(f^nw)<0$ for large $n$ and
$\sigma(w)=\lambda^{-n}\sigma(f^nw)<0$, as $\lambda\in(0,1)$.  For $w\in(L,L_2)$ the
same argument gives $\sigma(w)>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theorems}

\begin{theorem}[Confluence, general]\label{thm:gen-A}
Under \textup{(H1)--(H4)}, $\tau_n(\mu)\to B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}$ as $\mu\to\mu_0^-$ for every
$n\ge1$, with $\tau_n$ taken on the branch $\operatorname{Im}t_0=h/2$.  Under
\textup{(H5)} also $t_n/t_1^n\to B_n/B_1^n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sewing solution, general]\label{thm:gen-B}
Under \textup{(H1)--(H4)} there is $\mu_1<\mu_0$ such that for
$\mu\in(\mu_1,\mu_0)$ the following hold.  The sewn solution $\Kk^W_\mu$,
normalised by $\Kk^W_\mu(0)=w_0$, is the unique element of $\mathcal C_\mu(Y)$.  Its
coefficients satisfy $|c_n/\Lambda^n-\tau_n(\mu)|\le C_n\Lambda$, with $C_n$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:W2}(4) and constants $Y_B,Y_0,D,M$ that depend on the family
\textup{(}with $Y_0=Y_B+2+\pi|\rho|$; see the proof below\textup{)}.
The family is holomorphic in $\mu$ near $(\mu_1,\mu_0)$, and for $\operatorname{Im}\mu>0$
small it tends to $L$ and $L_2$ as $\operatorname{Im}z\to+\infty$ and $-\infty$,
respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[First and higher orders, general]\label{thm:gen-D}
Under \textup{(H1)--(H4)}, and for $n$ with $B_n\ne0$,
\[
 \log\frac{\tau_n}{B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}}\sim\sum_{j\ge1}\kappa^{(n)}_jp^j,\qquad
 \kappa^{(n)}_1=\frac{\delta t_n}{4a_2\gamma\,B_n}-\frac{2\pi\ii n\,\delta t_0}{4a_2\gamma},
\]
with $\delta t_m$ defined as in Theorem~\ref{thm:D} from the orbits of the germ $g$
and of its reflected local inverse.  Each $\kappa^{(n)}_j$ is given by convergent
parabolic series as in Theorem~\ref{thm:E}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:gen-A}--\ref{thm:gen-D}]
The proofs of Sections~\ref{sec:transition}--\ref{sec:char} apply verbatim,
with the following replacements.
\begin{itemize}
\item In Lemmas~\ref{lem:res}--\ref{lem:petal}, Theorem~\ref{thm:C} and
  Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}, replace
  $\alpha_2=-2/u+\tfrac13\log(-u)$ by $-1/(a_2u)+\rho\log(-u)$ and $-2/u$ by
  $-1/(a_2u)$.  The limiting petal is $\{\operatorname{Re}(-1/(a_2u))>R\}$.
\item The gate of Theorem~\ref{thm:C} needs a margin depending on $\rho$.  Put
  $c_\rho:=\pi|\rho|+1$.  On the gate,
  $\operatorname{Im}\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}-\operatorname{Im}(-1/(a_2u))
  =\rho\arg(-u)+O(1/Y_*)$, so $|\operatorname{Im}Z-\operatorname{Im}\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}|<c_\rho$
  for $\mu$ near $\mu_0$, and the gate lies in the band
  $\mathcal B_\mu(Y')$, $Y':=Y_*-c_\rho-2$, rather than $\mathcal B_\mu(Y_*-2)$.
  In step~(b) after the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}, fix
  $Y_*\ge Y_B+3c_\rho+3$, so that $Y'\ge Y_B+2c_\rho+1$.  For points within
  $o(1)$ of the gate, Lemma~\ref{lem:band}(ii) (with the constant of the next
  item) gives
  $|\operatorname{Im}\mathcal S_\mu|\le h/2-Y'+\pi|\rho|+\tfrac12+o(1)
  <h/2-Y'+c_\rho\le h/2-(Y_B+1)-c_\rho$.  So the shifted gate line lies in the
  strip $\Sigma$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:band}(iii) taken with $Y=Y_B+1$, where $T$ is
  holomorphic, and the rest of step~(b) is unchanged.
\item In Theorem~\ref{thm:C}(2), the reflected inverse
  $G_\mu(v)=-f_\mu^{-1}(-v)$ is the local inverse near $w^*$.  It is jointly
  holomorphic, its germ is $v+a_2v^2+(2a_2^2-a_3)v^3+\cdots$, and its iterative
  residue is $-\rho$.
\item In Lemma~\ref{lem:realT}, $1<L$ is replaced by \textup{(H4)}: $S$ maps
  $\mathbb R$ onto $(L,L_2)$, where $\sigma>0$, while $\sigma(w_0)<0$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-real}).
\item In Lemma~\ref{lem:band}, the bound $\pi/3$ in (ii) becomes $\pi|\rho|$
  (and $C/Y+C|\log\lambda|\le\tfrac12$ for $Y\ge Y_B$ and $\mu$ near $\mu_0$), and
  $h_2=h+2\pi/3+o(1)$ becomes $h_2=h+2\pi\rho+o(1)$.  Since $\pi|\rho|$ may exceed
  $2$, the half-width $h/2-Y-2$ of the strip in (iii) is replaced by
  $h/2-Y-c_\rho$, $\Sigma=\{|\operatorname{Im}w'|<h/2-Y-c_\rho\}$; the proof of
  (iii) is unchanged, because on the arcs
  $|\operatorname{Im}\mathcal S_\mu|\ge h/2-Y-\pi|\rho|-\tfrac12>h/2-Y-c_\rho$.  In
  (i) the drift $2\pi/3$ becomes $2\pi|\rho|$, and we take $Y_B\ge8c_\rho$.
  Accordingly, Lemma~\ref{lem:modes} holds with $Y_0:=Y_B+1+c_\rho$ in place of
  $Y_B+3$: with $Y=Y_B+1$, $\Sigma$ has half-width $h/2-Y_B-1-c_\rho=d+1$,
  $d=h/2-Y_0-1$.  Injectivity of $f_\mu$ on
  $D_{r'}$ follows from $f'_{\mu_0}(w^*)=1$ after shrinking $r'$.
\item In Proposition~\ref{prop:weld}, the singular set of $\Rr$ is the closed
  set $\Sigma_\Rr$ of inverse-branch images of the singular values of $f$.
\item In Lemma~\ref{lem:model}, $d_i=\tfrac i2u^{i-1}+\cdots$ becomes
  $d_i=ia_2u^{i-1}+\cdots$, and $D(s)=8s+\cdots$ becomes $4\gamma s/a_2+\cdots$.
  In Theorem~\ref{thm:D}, $p'(0)=2$ becomes $p'(0)=4a_2\gamma$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-scale}).
\item In Theorem~\ref{thm:W1}(2), $\lambda_{1,2}=1\mp a_2\sqrt{D(s)}+O(D)$ gives
  $\partial_\mu\lambda_1>0>\partial_\mu\lambda_2$, hence
  $\operatorname{Im}\mu>0\Rightarrow\arg\lambda_1>0>\arg\lambda_2$.
\item Statements involving ratios or logarithms use \textup{(H5)} in place of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:horn-nonzero}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:sewn-cusp-local}
  needs, in addition, that $w_0$ is not precritical for $f_{\mu_0}$.
\end{itemize}
Everything else is literally unchanged.  In particular the welding identity,
the Wiener-algebra contraction and the characterisation use only the two
Koenigs charts and the relation $\sigma\circ\Rr(w)=\sigma(w_0)\lambda^w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[On \textup{(H5)}]\label{rem:gen-B1}
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:horn-nonzero} gives $B_1\ne0$ whenever
$f_{\mu_0}$ restricted to its immediate parabolic basin has exactly one singular
value.  This covers $w^2+c$, whose basin contains the single critical point.
For $v-\sin v+c$ the critical values $2\pi k+1$ are real and only $k=0$ lies in
the basin, but the map is not of finite type, so applying the argument requires
a check.  For $w+(w^2-c)\eee^w$ we only know $B_1\ne0$ numerically.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Independence of the base point}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gen-base}
Let $w_0$ and $w_0'$ both satisfy \textup{(H4)}, and mark with a prime the
quantities computed from $w_0'$.  Then for every $\mu<\mu_0$ near $\mu_0$ and every
$n\ge1$,
\[
 \tau_n'(\mu)=\tau_n(\mu)\,\eee^{2\pi\ii n\Delta(\mu)},\qquad
 B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na'}=B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}\,\eee^{2\pi\ii n\Delta_0},
\]
with $\Delta(\mu)=\Rr_\mu^{-1}(w_0')$ and $\Delta_0=\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}(u^{*\prime})-\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}(u^*)$
both real, and $\Delta(\mu)\to\Delta_0$ as $\mu\to\mu_0^-$.  Hence $|\tau_n(\mu)|$ does not depend on the base point, and
neither does $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_j$ for any $j$.  The moduli $|c_n|$
and $|\hat c_n|$ of the sewn solution change only at relative order $O(n\Lambda)$.
\end{proposition}

The second identity is the definition $\Delta_0=a'-a$.  Its content is that
$\Delta_0$ is real and is the limit of $\Delta(\mu)$, so that the first identity
is compatible with Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-A}.  The independence of
$B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na}$ from the choice of coordinates is a different statement;
it is the conjugation invariance shown in the Setting.

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-real}, $\sigma(w_0)$ and $\sigma(w_0')$ are negative, so
$\Delta=\log(\sigma(w_0')/\sigma(w_0))/\log\lambda$ is real.  Then
$\Rr'^{-1}=\Rr^{-1}-\Delta$ and $T'=T-\Delta$, so $t_n'=t_n$ for $n\ne0$ and
$t_0'=t_0-\Delta$.  The branch condition $\operatorname{Im}t_0=h/2$ is preserved,
and $\tau_n'=\tau_n\eee^{2\pi\ii n\Delta}$.  At $\mu_0$, by \textup{(H4)} the
$g$-orbits of $u^*$ and $u^{*\prime}$ increase along the negative axis to $0$,
where $\alpha$ is real, so $a=\lim_k[\alpha(g^ku^*)-k]$ and $a'$ are real.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:C}(1) in the general form,
$\Delta(\mu)=\Rr_\mu^{-1}(w_0')\to\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}(u^{*\prime})-a=\Delta_0$.  Taking real
parts of the logarithm of the first identity divided by the second gives the
statement on $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_j$.  For the sewn solutions, the
normalisation $\Kk^W(0)=w_0'$ gives $\Kk^W[w_0']=\Kk^W[w_0](\cdot+\delta)$ with
$\delta+P(\delta)=\Delta$.  Since $\Kk^W$ is not real on the real axis,
$\operatorname{Im}\delta=-\operatorname{Im}P(\delta)=O(\Lambda)$ is in general not zero,
so by Remark~\ref{rem:invariance} $c_n$ is multiplied by
$\eee^{2\pi\ii n\delta}=\eee^{2\pi\ii n\operatorname{Re}\delta}(1+O(n\Lambda))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gen-invariant}
$|B_n|$ and the real parts $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_j$ are invariant under
orientation-preserving real-analytic conjugacy of the unfolding near
$(\mu_0,w^*)$ and under reparametrisation of $\mu$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $(\tilde f_{\tilde\mu},\tilde w_0)$ be a second unfolding satisfying
\textup{(H1)--(H4)}, $\tilde\mu=\theta(\mu)$ a real-analytic reparametrisation
with $\theta(\mu_0)=\tilde\mu_0$, $\theta'>0$, and $\phi_\mu$ univalent on a disc
$N$ about $w^*$, continuous in $\mu$, real with $\phi_\mu'>0$ on $N\cap\mathbb R$,
such that $\phi_\mu\circ f_\mu=\tilde f_{\theta(\mu)}\circ\phi_\mu$ on
$N\cap f_\mu^{-1}(N)$.  Fix $\mu<\mu_0$ near $\mu_0$ and write $\phi=\phi_\mu$,
$\tilde f=\tilde f_{\theta(\mu)}$.  Then $\phi(L)=\tilde L$ and
$\phi(L_2)=\tilde L_2$ with the same multipliers, so $h$, $\Lambda$ and $p$
agree; in particular $p$ depends only on the multipliers.

The base point $w_0$ need not lie in $N$.  Fix an auxiliary base point
$w_0'\in N$ with $w_0<w_0'<w^*$ and $[w_0',w^*]\subset N$; it satisfies
\textup{(H4)}, and for $\mu$ near $\mu_0$, $[w_0',L_2]\subset N$.

\emph{Attracting side.}  By uniqueness of Koenigs coordinates,
$\tilde\sigma=\phi'(L)\,\sigma\circ\phi^{-1}$ near $\tilde L$, and
$\sigma=\lambda^{-n}\sigma\circ f^n$ extends this to
$\tilde\sigma\circ\phi=\phi'(L)\,\sigma$ at every $w$ whose forward orbit stays
in $N$.  This includes $w_0'$ and a complex neighbourhood of $(L,L_2)$.  In
particular $\tilde\sigma(\phi(w_0'))=\phi'(L)\sigma(w_0')<0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-real}, and the attracting Abel coordinates with base points
$w_0'$ and $\phi(w_0')$ satisfy $\tilde\Rr'^{-1}\circ\phi=\Rr'^{-1}$ there.

\emph{Repelling side.}  Likewise
$\tilde\sigma_{\rm rep}=\phi'(L_2)\,\sigma_{\rm rep}\circ\phi^{-1}$ near $\tilde L_2$, i.e.\
$\tilde\sigma_{\rm rep}^{-1}(\zeta)=\phi\bigl(\sigma_{\rm rep}^{-1}(\zeta/\phi'(L_2))\bigr)$
for small $\zeta$.  Both sides continue along a neighbourhood of the negative
axis, because $\sigma_{\rm rep}^{-1}((-\infty,0))=(L,L_2)\subset N$.  Hence, for $z$
near $\mathbb R$,
\[
 \tilde S(z)=\tilde\sigma_{\rm rep}^{-1}\bigl(-\lambda_2^{\,z}\bigr)
 =\phi\bigl(S(z-c')\bigr),\qquad c'=\frac{\log\phi'(L_2)}{\log\lambda_2}\in\mathbb R .
\]

Combining, $\tilde T'(z)=\tilde\Rr'^{-1}(\tilde S(z))=T'(z-c')$ near $\mathbb R$,
where $T'$ and $\tilde T'$ are computed from $w_0'$ and $\phi(w_0')$.  Since
$c'$ is real, both sides lie on the branch $\operatorname{Im}t_0=h/2$, and by
the translation invariance~\eqref{eq:taun}, $\tilde\tau_n'=\tau_n'$.  The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-base} uses only $\sigma<0$ at the two base points at
the given $\mu$; applied to $f$ with $w_0,w_0'$ and to $\tilde f$ with
$\tilde w_0,\phi(w_0')$, it gives
$|\tau_n|=|\tau_n'|=|\tilde\tau_n'|=|\tilde\tau_n|$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-A} and $a\in\mathbb R$, $|B_n|=\lim|\tau_n|$, so
$|\tilde B_n|=|B_n|$.  Since $a$ is real,
$\operatorname{Re}\log(\tau_n/B_n\eee^{2\pi\ii na})=\log|\tau_n|-\log|B_n|$, which is
therefore the same function of $p$ for both unfoldings.  As $p$ is increasing
in $s$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-scale}), the coefficients of the asymptotic
expansion in $p$ are unique, and $\operatorname{Re}\tilde\kappa^{(n)}_j=\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_j$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The first-order term as a derivative on the parabolic stratum}
\label{sec:gen-variation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-D} gives $\kappa^{(n)}$ as a convergent series.  Here we
identify it.  It is the one-sided derivative of a function that extends
continuously to the parabolic maps, and on those maps the function is the horn
coefficient.  So $\kappa^{(n)}$ splits into one number attached to the germ and
the derivative of the horn map along the unfolding.

Work in the affine coordinate $u$ of the Setting, $w=w^*+cu$.  Let $\mathcal O_r$ be the Banach space of
bounded holomorphic functions on $D_r=\{|u|<r\}$ with the supremum norm.  An
unfolding in this subsection is a real-analytic curve
$s\mapsto f_s\in\mathcal O_r$ with $f_0=g$ and the sign condition of
\textup{(H3)}.  No global hypothesis such as \textup{(H1)} is needed: $\tau_n$
denotes the invariant $t_n\eee^{-2\pi\ii nt_0}$ of the local transition
$\mathcal A\circ(\mathcal S^\sharp)^{-1}$ on the gate line, which is all that
Theorems~\ref{thm:reduction}--\ref{thm:E} use.  We write
\[
 f_s=g-sX+O(s^2),\qquad X\in\mathcal O_r,\qquad X(0)=\gamma>0 .
\]
Call $X$ the \emph{direction} of the unfolding.  The radius $r$ is fixed
small enough that all compositions below ($f\circ g$, $\phi\circ g$ for
$\phi\in\mathcal O_{2r}$) are defined on a neighbourhood of $\overline{D_{r/2}}$,
where all estimates take place.  Let $\mathcal P$ be the set of
$f\in\mathcal O_r$ near $g$ with a double fixed point in $D_{r/2}$ (the
\emph{parabolic stratum}).  Near $g$, $\mathcal P$ is the zero set of the
discriminant $D[f]=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2$ of the Weierstrass polynomial of
$f-\mathrm{id}$.  Since $\sigma_1[g]=\sigma_2[g]=0$ and
$\sigma_2[f]=f(0)/a_2+O(\|f-g\|^2)$, $D$ is a submersion at $g$.  Hence
$\mathcal P$ is a complex-analytic hypersurface with tangent space
\[
 T_g\mathcal P=\{Y\in\mathcal O_r:\ Y(0)=0\},
\]
where a tangent vector $Y$ means the curve $g-tY+O(t^2)$.  Every
$Y\in T_g\mathcal P$ is tangent to a holomorphic curve in $\mathcal P$, for instance
$g-tY+c(t)$ with $c(t)=O(t^2)$ given by the implicit function theorem; for
real $Y$ the curve is real.

For real $f$ near $g$ with two real fixed points (attracting one on the left),
put $\mathcal I_n[f]=\tau_n[f]$.  For $f\in\mathcal P$ put
$\mathcal I_n[f]=B_n[f]\,\eee^{2\pi\ii na[f]}$, computed from the germ of $f$ at its
double fixed point with the same base point $w_0$.  Both are the invariant
$t_n\eee^{-2\pi\ii nt_0}$ of $T=\mathcal A[f]\circ\mathcal S^\sharp[f]^{-1}$ on
the gate line, with $\mathcal A[f]$ normalised by $\mathcal A[f](u^*)=0$
(Theorems~\ref{thm:reduction} and~\ref{thm:D}).  This normalisation matters:
an additive constant on the attracting side changes $t_n\eee^{-2\pi\ii nt_0}$,
while one on the repelling side does not.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-A}, $\mathcal I_n$ is continuous at $g$ from the side of two
real fixed points.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gen-banach}
Fix $N\ge3$.  After shrinking $r$, the coefficient $\nu[f]=A_1+A_2$ and the
one-step error $F[f]=\Psi[f]\circ f-\Psi[f]-1=q[f]^N\Gamma[f]$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:model} are holomorphic functions of $f$ on a neighbourhood of
$g$ in $\mathcal O_r$, with values in bounded holomorphic functions on
$D_{r/2}$.  For each $\delta\in(0,r/2)$, the model time $\Psi[f]-\nu[f]\log u$
is holomorphic in $f$ on a (smaller) neighbourhood of $g$, with values in
bounded holomorphic functions on the annulus $\delta<|u|<r/2$.  The same
holds for the reflected inverse $G[f](v)=-f^{-1}(-v)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:model} uses only Weierstrass preparation and
division, the removability of the symmetric expressions $\Theta$, $\Theta'$
across the diagonal, and the inversion of the triangular matrix $M(0)$.  Each
of these steps depends holomorphically on a Banach parameter.  The inverse
function theorem gives $G[f]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gen-linear}
There is a continuous complex-linear functional $\mathfrak L_n$ on $\mathcal O_r$ with
the following two properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every unfolding with direction $X$,
  $\mathcal I_n[f_s]=\mathcal I_n[g]+s\,\mathfrak L_n(X)+O(s^{10/9})$ as
  $s\to0^+$.
\item For every holomorphic curve $t\mapsto g_t\in\mathcal P$ with $g_0=g$, the
  function $t\mapsto\mathcal I_n[g_t]$ is holomorphic near $0$, with derivative
  $\mathfrak L_n(-\partial_tg_t|_{t=0})$ at $0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Define $\mathfrak L_n(X)$ by the formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:D} for
$\dot\tau_n$, where every derivative in $s$ is replaced by the derivative in the
direction $-X$:
\[
 \dot{\mathcal A}(u)=d\Psi[g](-X)(u)
 +\sum_{k\ge0}\Bigl[dF[g](-X)(u_k)+F[g]'(u_k)\,\dot u_k\Bigr],\qquad
 \dot u_{k+1}=g'(u_k)\dot u_k-X(u_k),\ \dot u_0=0,
\]
on the compact set $K\subset P_R$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:diff}; at the base
point and on the rest of the basin it is continued by
$\dot{\mathcal A}=\dot{\mathcal A}\circ g^m+\mathcal A_0'\circ g^m\cdot\dot u_m$,
as in Section~\ref{sec:first}.  Define $\dot{\mathcal S}^\sharp$ in the same way from
$G$, with the constant $\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}[g](p')$ of $\mathcal S^\sharp$ held
fixed, and then $\delta T$, $\delta t_m$ and
$\mathfrak L_n(X)=\eee^{-2\pi\ii nt_0}(\delta t_n-2\pi\ii n\,t_n\,\delta t_0)$ at $g$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-banach} every term is linear in $X$.  The bounds of
Lemma~\ref{lem:derbounds} hold with $\|X\|$ as a factor, so the series converge
and $\mathfrak L_n$ is continuous.

(1) For a single unfolding, $\partial_s\Psi_s|_{s=0}=d\Psi[g](-X)$ and
$\partial_\sigma F_\sigma|_{\sigma=0}=dF[g](-X)$ by the chain rule, so
$\dot{\mathcal A}$ above is the derivative of Proposition~\ref{prop:diff}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} (in the form of Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-D}) is statement~(1).

(2) Let $t\mapsto g_t\in\mathcal P$ be a holomorphic curve with $g_0=g$, with
double fixed point $u_0(t)$.  The germ of $g_t$ at $u_0(t)$ has
$a_2(t)\to a_2$, so the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} with $\sigma=0$, in the
coordinate $-1/(a_2(t)(u-u_0(t)))$, gives the petal bounds for all $k$,
uniformly for small complex $t$.  Hence
$\mathcal A[g_t](u)=\Psi[g_t](u)+\sum_kF[g_t](g_t^ku)$ converges uniformly and
is holomorphic in $t$.  By Weierstrass' theorem its $t$-derivative is the
termwise derivative, which is the series for $\dot{\mathcal A}$ with $-X$
replaced by $\partial_tg_t|_{t=0}$.  As at $s=0$ in
Section~\ref{sec:first}, $\mathcal A[g_t]$ is $\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}[g_t]$ plus a
constant; this constant is removed by the normalisation
$\mathcal A[g_t](u^*)=0$, so that $\mathcal A[g_t]-\mathcal A[g_t](u^*)=\Phi_{\mathrm{att}}[g_t]-a[g_t]$.
The repelling side is the same with $G$, and there additive constants do not
affect $\mathcal I_n$.  The inversion and the Fourier integrals are holomorphic
in $t$, so $\mathcal I_n[g_t]$ is holomorphic with derivative
$\mathfrak L_n(-\partial_tg_t|_{t=0})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Variation formula]\label{thm:gen-variation}
Assume $B_n\ne0$.  Put
\[
 \kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(g)=\frac{\mathfrak L_n(1)}{4a_2\,\mathcal I_n[g]},\qquad
 \mathcal D_n(g)[Y]=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\log\mathcal I_n[g_t]\quad(Y\in T_g\mathcal P),
\]
where $g_t\in\mathcal P$ is any holomorphic curve with $g_0=g$ and
$\partial_tg_t|_{t=0}=-Y$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-linear}(2),
$\mathcal D_n(g)[Y]=\mathfrak L_n(Y)/\mathcal I_n[g]$, so $\mathcal D_n(g)$ is well defined
and linear.
Then for every unfolding of $g$ with direction $X$,
\[
 \kappa^{(n)}=\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(g)+\frac{\mathcal D_n(g)\bigl[X-X(0)\bigr]}{4a_2\,X(0)} .
\]
In particular $\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(g)$ is the first-order coefficient of the
translation unfolding $g-s$, and
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}=\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(g)
 +\frac{d\log|B_n|\,\bigl[X-X(0)\bigr]}{4a_2\,X(0)},
\]
where $d\log|B_n|[Y]=\frac{d}{dt}\log|B_n[g_t]|$ along real curves in $\mathcal P$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-scale}, $p=4a_2\gamma s+O(s^2)$ with
$\gamma=X(0)$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-linear}(1) and $B_n\ne0$,
$\kappa^{(n)}=\mathfrak L_n(X)/(4a_2\gamma\,\mathcal I_n[g])$.  Write
$X=\gamma\cdot1+(X-\gamma)$.  Then $X-\gamma\in T_g\mathcal P$, and
by definition $\mathfrak L_n(X-\gamma)=\mathcal I_n[g]\,\mathcal D_n(g)[X-\gamma]$.  By linearity,
\[
 \kappa^{(n)}=\frac{\gamma\,\mathfrak L_n(1)+\mathcal I_n[g]\,\mathcal D_n(g)[X-\gamma]}
 {4a_2\gamma\,\mathcal I_n[g]} .
\]
For the real part, let $f\in\mathcal P$ be real.  Its double fixed point is
real and the base point lies on the real attracting axis, so $a[f]$ is real
and $|\mathcal I_n[f]|=|B_n[f]|$.  For real $X$, take a real curve $g_t$ in
$\mathcal P$ tangent to $X-\gamma$.  Along it
$\operatorname{Re}\log\mathcal I_n[g_t]=\log|B_n[g_t]|$, so
$\operatorname{Re}\mathcal D_n(g)[X-\gamma]=d\log|B_n|\,[X-\gamma]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gen-variation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}$ depends on $X$ only through its class
  modulo the infinitesimal conjugacies $L\phi=\phi\circ g-g'\phi$, $\phi$ real.
  Numerically, it is not a function of the germ alone
  (Remark~\ref{rem:gen-variation}).
\item For tetration the direction is $X=(1+u)\eee^u$, and $X-1=L(-2-u)$.
  Hence $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}=\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(\eee^u-1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(1) $L\phi(0)=0$, so $X\mapsto X+L\phi$ keeps $X(0)$ and adds $L\phi\in T_g\mathcal P$
to $X-X(0)$.  The curve $(\mathrm{id}+t\phi)^{-1}\circ g\circ(\mathrm{id}+t\phi)$
lies in $\mathcal P$ and equals $g-tL\phi+O(t^2)$, so its direction is $L\phi$.
Along it $|B_n|$ is constant: the Fatou coordinates are transported by the
real conjugacy, which changes their $\alpha$-normalisation by a real
constant $C$, and $B_n\mapsto B_n\eee^{-2\pi\ii nC}$ preserves $|B_n|$.  So
$d\log|B_n|\,[L\phi]=0$.
(2) $L(-2-u)=-(\eee^u-1)-2+(2+u)\eee^u=(1+u)\eee^u-1$.  Apply~(1).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The constant $\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}$ is a cocycle]\label{rem:gen-cocycle}
Let $\psi$ be real-analytic near $0$ with $\psi(0)=0$, $\psi'(0)=1$, and put
$\tilde g=\psi^{-1}\circ g\circ\psi$.  Conjugating the translation unfolding
$g-s$ by $\psi$ gives $\psi^{-1}\circ(g-s)\circ\psi=\tilde g-s\tilde X+O(s^2)$
with $\tilde X=(1/\psi')\circ\tilde g$, and $\tilde X(0)=1$.  Take the base point
in the domain of $\psi$; by Proposition~\ref{prop:gen-base} this does not affect
real parts.  Corollary~\ref{cor:gen-invariant}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-variation} give
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(\tilde g)=\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}(g)
 -\frac{d\log|B_n|(\tilde g)\,[\tilde X-1]}{4a_2} .
\]
So $\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}$ is not an invariant of the germ: it depends on the
choice of the transverse direction $1$, which is not preserved by changes of
coordinates.  The invariant object is the affine function
$X\mapsto\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}[g;X]$ on directions normalised by $X(0)=1$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:gen-variation} describes it completely: its linear part is
$d\log|B_n|$ on the parabolic stratum, and its value on one transverse
direction is $\operatorname{Re}\kappa^{(n)}_{\rm tr}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Numerical evidence for the analogue of Theorem~\ref{mthm:F} for $w^2+c$]\label{rem:gen-quad}
For $f_c(w)=w^2+c$ the parameter $c=1/4$ is the cusp of the main cardioid, which
plays the role of the Shell--Thron region.  For real $c>1/4$ the two fixed points
are complex conjugate and repelling, and the real axis escapes.  The
two-fixed-point construction of Kneser type converges at the sampled
parameters with the normalisation $F(0)=f_c^3(0)$, corresponding to the intended
critical-point marking $F(-3)=0$ (script \texttt{quad\_kneser.py}).
The separate proof document \texttt{docs/proofs/quadratic-continuation.tex}
establishes its quadratic F and G using marked tilted domains, an explicit
polynomial multiplier deformation with open-overlap comparison, and an
independently replayed 4,013-box Arb exterior cover. Its conclusion concerns
regular height germs with this normalisation; it does not imply convergence
of the present numerical iteration. The following earlier observations
provide numerical evidence only.
\begin{itemize}
\item Along the arc $c=\tfrac14+0.1\eee^{\ii\varphi}$ the sampled values are
  consistent with continuation across the cardioid boundary.  Interpolation
  through points on both sides of the
  neutral band predicts held-out points to $2\cdot10^{-8}$, as accurately as
  interpolation within one side.
\item On vertical ladders $c=c_r+\ii y$, extrapolation towards $y=0$ supports
  agreement with the sewn coefficient.  At $\lambda=0.1$ the extrapolated value gives
  $(\hat c_1/\tau_1-1)/\Lambda=0.2272-0.1120\,\ii$, against $0.2279-0.1115\,\ii$
  from the first-order formula at the end of Section~\ref{sec:W2}.  So the
  data support agreement with $\Kk^W$ including its $O(\Lambda)$ correction;
  they do not prove existence or identification of the boundary limit.  The ratio
  $c_2/c_1^2$ agrees with $t_2/t_1^2$ to $4\cdot10^{-8}$.
\end{itemize}
For the analogue of Theorem~\ref{mthm:G} the numerics are only partial.  The
construction converges at sampled points in a wedge to the lower right of the
cusp.  In the multiplier coordinate $c=\mu/2-\mu^2/4$, at
$\arg\mu=0.25\pi$ and $0.4\pi$, interpolation across the neutral band errs by
$\le10^{-7}$ against a variation of order $10^{-1}$.  Further left the iteration
does not converge, and above it converges only after the $\theta$ lines are
raised in some tested cases.  These observations are consistent with local
holomorphic continuation, but do not establish it. Moving the Taylor centre
or fixing the constant Fourier mode of $\theta_{\mathrm{up}}$ may enlarge
the regular numerical region; these changes remain untested. They cannot
alone give direct coverage of the whole upper half-plane while retaining
both Schr\"oder charts: at $c=1/4+i/2$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}=i$, the
fifth inverse-Schr\"oder coefficient requires $0=(3-5i)/2$.
The separate quadratic proof document supplies the exact obstruction and
a conditional local convergence theorem. A full numerical method must
also replace the neutral-end representation.
A pointwise algorithm that does not record paths does not by itself establish
single-valued analytic continuation; parameter holomorphy and agreement of
local branches must also be established.
\end{remark}
